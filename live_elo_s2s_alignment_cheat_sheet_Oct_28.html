Naturalness / Engagement / Aesthetics

Does it sound human? Is it engaging, like a close friend? Is the delivery authentic? Always judge relative to the target locale (e.g. how natural it feels to a Mexican Spanish speaker for es_MX).

Belongs here

  • Robotic or monotone delivery, unnatural pitch, flat emotion, “reading aloud”
  • Unnatural breath sounds (per the FAQ), plus the Overacted cluster if applicable
  • Non-native accent or wrong variant (also flag the Wrong Accent/Variant cluster)
  • Wrong honorifics or register (e.g. Korean politeness levels)
  • Mispronunciations, and fillers used naturally or unnaturally

Not NEA

  • Interruptions, latency, yielding the floor → CD
  • Clicks, pops, distortion, persona shift → AQ

Evidence that's enough

Pattern-based descriptions are fine: “robotic”, “monotone”, “English-accented”, plus their scope. “More natural” on its own is not enough.

Conversational Dynamics

The timing and structure of turn-taking: when to speak, yield, or interrupt; how pauses, barge-ins and backchannels (“uh-huh”) are handled; avoiding dead air and turn-hoarding.

  • Tie by default when neither model has a real turn-taking event.
  • Pick a winner from observable events: interruptions (turn), latency (timeline seconds), abandoned turns.
  • Skip only when there's not enough interaction to judge (e.g. a one-turn conversation).
  • Use the Timeline view to verify overlaps and pauses.

Common confusions

  • “Smoother flow” or “natural tone” describes NEA, not CD.
  • A brief backchannel that doesn't disrupt isn't an interruption. An “um” spoken over the user mid-turn is.
  • A 1–3s pause is Minor. Don't let it decide CD or a deduction.
  • Network vs. model latency: if both models lag the same way, or the audio is choppy in both directions, it's likely network, so don't flag Latency.

Task Success & Error Clusters

Error Cluster Miss is −1 and doesn't stack (Task Success included). Enforcement covers Moderate and Major only, though repeated Minor issues can add up to Moderate. Explain every flagged cluster in the rationale.

Task Success: Pass / Partial / Fail #1 missed (598)

  • Did the model fulfil every layer of the request: explicit asks, implicit expectations and emotional needs (e.g. venting needs validation, not a 5-step plan)?
  • Partial when some elements are clearly done and others neglected (e.g. the answer was right but the requested sleepy register was dropped).
  • Wrong language: Partial if the model switched back by itself or after one correction. Fail if it didn't switch, or needed more than one correction.
  • Factual errors belong to Utility and Inaccuracy. They don't automatically fail Task Success.

Naturalness / Engagement / Aesthetics

Does it sound human? Is it engaging, like a close friend? Is the delivery authentic? Always judge relative to the target locale (e.g. how natural it feels to a Mexican Spanish speaker for es_MX).

Belongs here

  • Robotic or monotone delivery, unnatural pitch, flat emotion, “reading aloud”
  • Unnatural breath sounds (per the FAQ), plus the Overacted cluster if applicable
  • Non-native accent or wrong variant (also flag the Wrong Accent/Variant cluster)
  • Wrong honorifics or register (e.g. Korean politeness levels)
  • Mispronunciations, and fillers used naturally or unnaturally

Not NEA

  • Interruptions, latency, yielding the floor → CD
  • Clicks, pops, distortion, persona shift → AQ

Evidence that's enough

Pattern-based descriptions are fine: “robotic”, “monotone”, “English-accented”, plus their scope. “More natural” on its own is not enough.

Conversational Dynamics

The timing and structure of turn-taking: when to speak, yield, or interrupt; how pauses, barge-ins and backchannels (“uh-huh”) are handled; avoiding dead air and turn-hoarding.

  • Tie by default when neither model has a real turn-taking event.
  • Pick a winner from observable events: interruptions (turn), latency (timeline seconds), abandoned turns.
  • Skip only when there's not enough interaction to judge (e.g. a one-turn conversation).
  • Use the Timeline view to verify overlaps and pauses.

Common confusions

  • “Smoother flow” or “natural tone” describes NEA, not CD.
  • A brief backchannel that doesn't disrupt isn't an interruption. An “um” spoken over the user mid-turn is.
  • A 1–3s pause is Minor. Don't let it decide CD or a deduction.
  • Network vs. model latency: if both models lag the same way, or the audio is choppy in both directions, it's likely network, so don't flag Latency.

Conversational Dynamics

The timing and structure of turn-taking: when to speak, yield, or interrupt; how pauses, barge-ins and backchannels (“uh-huh”) are handled; avoiding dead air and turn-hoarding.

  • Tie by default when neither model has a real turn-taking event.

  • Skip only when there's not enough interaction to judge (e.g. a one-turn conversation).

Conversational Dynamics

The timing and structure of turn-taking: when to speak, yield, or interrupt; how pauses, barge-ins and backchannels (“uh-huh”) are handled; avoiding dead air and turn-hoarding.

  • Tie by default when neither model has a real turn-taking event.
  • Pick a winner from observable events: interruptions (turn), latency (timeline seconds), abandoned turns.
  • Skip only when there's not enough interaction to judge (e.g. a one-turn conversation).
  • Use the Timeline view to verify overlaps and pauses.

Common confusions

  • “Smoother flow” or “natural tone” describes NEA, not CD.
  • A brief backchannel that doesn't disrupt isn't an interruption. An “um” spoken over the user mid-turn is.
  • A 1–3s pause is Minor. Don't let it decide CD or a deduction.
  • Network vs. model latency: if both models lag the same way, or the audio is choppy in both directions, it's likely network, so don't flag Latency.

Naturalness / Engagement / Aesthetics

Does it sound human? Is it engaging, like a close friend? Is the delivery authentic? Always judge relative to the target locale (e.g. how natural it feels to a Mexican Spanish speaker for es_MX).

Belongs here

  • Robotic or monotone delivery, unnatural pitch, flat emotion, “reading aloud”

  • Wrong honorifics or register (e.g. Korean politeness levels)
  • Mispronunciations, and fillers used naturally or unnaturally

Not NEA

  • Interruptions, latency, yielding the floor → CD

Conversational Dynamics

The timing and structure of turn-taking: when to speak, yield, or interrupt; how pauses, barge-ins and backchannels (“uh-huh”) are handled; avoiding dead air and turn-hoarding.

  • Tie by default when neither model has a real turn-taking event.
  • Pick a winner from observable events: interruptions (turn), latency (timeline seconds), abandoned turns.
  • Skip only when there's not enough interaction to judge (e.g. a one-turn conversation).
  • Use the Timeline view to verify overlaps and pauses.

Common confusions

  • “Smoother flow” or “natural tone” describes NEA, not CD.
  • A brief backchannel that doesn't disrupt isn't an interruption. An “um” spoken over the user mid-turn is.
  • A 1–3s pause is Minor. Don't let it decide CD or a deduction.
  • Network vs. model latency: if both models lag the same way, or the audio is choppy in both directions, it's likely network, so don't flag Latency.

Conversational Dynamics

The timing and structure of turn-taking: when to speak, yield, or interrupt; how pauses, barge-ins and backchannels (“uh-huh”) are handled; avoiding dead air and turn-hoarding.

  • Tie by default when neither model has a real turn-taking event.
  • Pick a winner from observable events: interruptions (turn), latency (timeline seconds), abandoned turns.
  • Skip only when there's not enough interaction to judge (e.g. a one-turn conversation).
  • Use the Timeline view to verify overlaps and pauses.

Common confusions

  • “Smoother flow” or “natural tone” describes NEA, not CD.

Conversational Dynamics

The timing and structure of turn-taking: when to speak, yield, or interrupt; how pauses, barge-ins and backchannels (“uh-huh”) are handled; avoiding dead air and turn-hoarding.

  • Tie by default when neither model has a real turn-taking event.
  • Pick a winner from observable events: interruptions (turn), latency (timeline seconds), abandoned turns.
  • Skip only when there's not enough interaction to judge (e.g. a one-turn conversation).
  • Use the Timeline view to verify overlaps and pauses.

Common confusions

  • “Smoother flow” or “natural tone” describes NEA, not CD.
  • A brief backchannel that doesn't disrupt isn't an interruption. An “um” spoken over the user mid-turn is.
  • A 1–3s pause is Minor. Don't let it decide CD or a deduction.
  • Network vs. model latency: if both models lag the same way, or the audio is choppy in both directions, it's likely network, so don't flag Latency.

Conversational Dynamics

The timing and structure of turn-taking: when to speak, yield, or interrupt; how pauses, barge-ins and backchannels (“uh-huh”) are handled; avoiding dead air and turn-hoarding.

  • Tie by default when neither model has a real turn-taking event.
  • Pick a winner from observable events: interruptions (turn), latency (timeline seconds), abandoned turns.
  • Skip only when there's not enough interaction to judge (e.g. a one-turn conversation).
  • Use the Timeline view to verify overlaps and pauses.

Common confusions

  • “Smoother flow” or “natural tone” describes NEA, not CD.
  • A brief backchannel that doesn't disrupt isn't an interruption. An “um” spoken over the user mid-turn is.
  • A 1–3s pause is Minor. Don't let it decide CD or a deduction.
  • Network vs. model latency: if both models lag the same way, or the audio is choppy in both directions, it's likely network, so don't flag Latency.

Conversational Dynamics

The timing and structure of turn-taking: when to speak, yield, or interrupt; how pauses, barge-ins and backchannels (“uh-huh”) are handled; avoiding dead air and turn-hoarding.

  • Tie by default when neither model has a real turn-taking event.
  • Pick a winner from observable events: interruptions (turn), latency (timeline seconds), abandoned turns.
  • Skip only when there's not enough interaction to judge (e.g. a one-turn conversation).
  • Use the Timeline view to verify overlaps and pauses.

  • “Smoother flow” or “natural tone” describes NEA, not CD.

Naturalness / Engagement / Aesthetics

Does it sound human? Is it engaging, like a close friend? Is the delivery authentic? Always judge relative to the target locale (e.g. how natural it feels to a Mexican Spanish speaker for es_MX).

Belongs here

  • Robotic or monotone delivery, unnatural pitch, flat emotion, “reading aloud”
  • Unnatural breath sounds (per the FAQ), plus the Overacted cluster if applicable
  • Non-native accent or wrong variant (also flag the Wrong Accent/Variant cluster)
  • Wrong honorifics or register (e.g. Korean politeness levels)
  • Mispronunciations, and fillers used naturally or unnaturally

Not NEA

  • Interruptions, latency, yielding the floor → CD
  • Clicks, pops, distortion, persona shift → AQ

Evidence that's enough

Pattern-based descriptions are fine: “robotic”, “monotone”, “English-accented”, plus their scope. “More natural” on its own is not enough.

Conversational Dynamics

The timing and structure of turn-taking: when to speak, yield, or interrupt; how pauses, barge-ins and backchannels (“uh-huh”) are handled; avoiding dead air and turn-hoarding.

  • Tie by default when neither model has a real turn-taking event.
  • Pick a winner from observable events: interruptions (turn), latency (timeline seconds), abandoned turns.
  • Skip only when there's not enough interaction to judge (e.g. a one-turn conversation).
  • Use the Timeline view to verify overlaps and pauses.

Common confusions

  • “Smoother flow” or “natural tone” describes NEA, not CD.
  • A brief backchannel that doesn't disrupt isn't an interruption. An “um” spoken over the user mid-turn is.
  • A 1–3s pause is Minor. Don't let it decide CD or a deduction.
  • Network vs. model latency: if both models lag the same way, or the audio is choppy in both directions, it's likely network, so don't flag Latency.
