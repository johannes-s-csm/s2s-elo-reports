If you're still unsure after a replay, it doesn't count. In the last 7 days, 33% of AQ feedback still used severity framing (even on submissions from Oct 21 onward), and 841 submissions had AQ cited under Rating Dimension Quality. Under the new audit rule that deduction no longer exists: AQ is capped at -1 JQ.

How to decide, in 2 steps

Listen in a quiet room with wired headphones (3.5 mm if Bluetooth/USB adds static), at 50–75% volume and 1× speed.

1

Judge each model independently

Does this model have a defect I can clearly hear on the 1st or 2nd listen?
  • If unsure, replay that segment once. A real defect is at the same spot every time; a playback glitch that disappears doesn't count.
  • Don't keep replaying to hunt for barely perceptible issues.
2

Compare: how many models have a clear defect?

NeitherTie (Both Good)
Only AB Preferred
Only BA Preferred
Both, one clearly less affectedPrefer the less-affected model
Both, neither clearly betterTie (Both Bad)

Weigh frequency, duration and intrusiveness, not raw counts. One intrusive defect can matter more than several brief ones. Never force a preference on a barely perceptible difference.

🚫 Catastrophic override

An output with any of these can never be Good or Preferred:
  • Some or all words can't be understood at all (slightly muffled or slurred but understandable speech doesn't count)
  • Background noise is as loud as or louder than the model's voice
  • A secondary talker is audible

If only one output is catastrophic, prefer the other. If both are, select Both Bad.

Is it AQ? Where each issue goes

AQ covers technical defects in the model's output audio only. These are the most common mis-routings seen this week.

Writing the AQ rationale

A / B Preferred required

Describe the defects (or their absence) in both models and explain the comparison.

AQ: MB preferred. MA has clicks in T2 (0:04) and T6 (0:08). MB has one brief click in T4 (0:02), so it's less affected overall.
Both Bad required

Name the defects in both models and say why neither has a clear advantage, or name the catastrophic condition.

AQ: Both Bad. MA has distortion in T4 (0:05–0:15). MB has a strong echo in T4 (0:05) and a glitch in T6 (0:02). The effect is comparable.
Both Good optional

A short note or nothing at all is fine. Just make sure nothing else in the rationale contradicts it (e.g. "MB sounded crackly").

AQ: Both Good. No audible defects in either model.

✅ Do

  • Model + turn + timestamp for each discrete defect
  • Use 2–3 representative examples for repeated defects
  • Name the word where it happens (e.g. pop on "picking", T3 0:02)
  • Check the UI selection against your AQ sentence before submitting

❌ Don't

  • "Model A sounded better" (a conclusion only, which gets -1 JQ)
  • Describe only one model for A/B Preferred or Both Bad
  • Add Major / Moderate / Minor labels (not needed)
  • Use dB meters or waveform tools to decide AQ
  • Let AQ reasoning creep into NEA, CD or Utility (or the other way round)

For auditors & LAs

Check the evidence, not the winner

  • No -2 Dimension Quality deductions for AQ, whether for errors, severity disagreements or a different AQ selection.
  • Max -1 JQ, only when the required AQ explanation is: missing · too vague to verify · one-sided · contradicts the label · cites an issue you can't hear at that model, turn and timestamp.
  • A reasonable AQ disagreement is not a JQ issue.

Don't deduct when…

  • The cited defects are audible and support the label, even if you'd pick differently
  • The annotator missed an issue, including a catastrophic one. Coach in the overall comment instead.
  • Both Good has no explanation, or only a brief one
  • The artifact is misnamed, or severity labels are missing
  • The rationale is concise but verifiable
